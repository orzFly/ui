## Content first, chrome quiet

**People live in these tools all day; the interface recedes so the work stays in front.**

A log, a diff, a list of cards is what someone opened the app for. Toolbars, borders and badges are
there to serve it, so they take the muted colours, the small sizes and the edges of the screen.
Give emphasis to one thing per view, the action most people came to take, and let everything else
stay calm. Every extra border, shadow or colour competes with the content for the same attention.

A conversation from Mail. The subject and the message carry the weight; the toolbar is ghost icons
in the muted colour, and **Send** is the one filled button.

## Dense with a pointer, generous with a thumb

**One component, two sizes, chosen by the pointer.**

A mouse can hit a 32px button all day, and a dense screen shows more of the work at once. A thumb
cannot, so on a coarse pointer the same components grow their hit areas by themselves. Write one
layout and let the components size themselves; do not branch the app on screen width to pass bigger
sizes. [Density](/guidelines/foundations/density/) has the sizes and when to step down from them.

<Example of={MailSelection} />

Press **Touch** under the example: the same toolbar and rows grow for a thumb, with no change to
the code.

## Nothing only on hover

**Every action is reachable by keyboard and by touch.**

Hover is a hint, never the only way in. A row's actions are on screen, or behind a visible `⋯`
menu, rather than waiting for a hover that a keyboard and a thumb cannot give. A tooltip repeats a
label the control already carries; it never holds the only copy. Every control can be reached with
Tab and used with the keys a native control would take.

<Example of={ReachableActions} />

Tab through Mail's thread toolbar. Each tooltip opens on focus as it does on hover, and repeats the
button's name with its shortcut; **More actions** opens with Enter or Space.

## Undo over confirm

**Confirm only what cannot be undone.**

A confirmation dialog asks a question people learn to answer without reading. For an action that
can be taken back, act at once and offer **Undo** in a toast. Keep the dialog for the few that cannot:
deleting what no one can restore, sending what cannot be unsent. Then name the thing and the
consequence, and make the destructive button say what it does.

<Example of={UndoOrConfirm} />

Archive the conversation: it goes at once, and **Undo** in the toast brings it back. Revoking the
token cannot be taken back, so Settings asks first, and names the token and what stops working.

## The theme is the user's

**No hard-coded colours; every surface reads the tokens.**

People pick a theme to read comfortably, in a dark room, in sunlight, with low vision. An app that
writes `#fff` or `text-gray-500` breaks that choice in one place and nobody notices until a user in
a dark theme does. Draw with the [colour tokens](/guidelines/foundations/color/), and let
[theming](/guidelines/foundations/theming/) do the rest.

<Example of={ThemedChecks} />

The checks of a pull request's CI run, drawn only with tokens. Pick another theme from the site's
theme menu, light or dark, and every surface, border and status colour follows.

## Say what happened

**Loading, empty, failed and done each look different and say so.**

A blank area could mean any of four things, and the user should never have to guess which. Loading
shows the shape of what is coming; empty says there is nothing yet and how to add something; failed
says what went wrong and offers a retry; done says so once, briefly, and gets out of the way. A
state that cannot be told apart from another is a bug.
