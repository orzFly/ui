These guidelines are for the apps built on @orz/ui: tools people keep open all day, used at a desk
with a mouse and a keyboard, and on a phone with a thumb. Storybook says what each component takes;
these pages say when to reach for it, how big it is on each kind of pointer, and what to do around
it.

## How a page reads

Each page opens with its rule in one sentence. A live example follows, built from the real
components, with the file it came from one tab away; **Pointer** and **Touch** under it preview
either kind of pointer on any device. Then come Do and Don't pairs, a decision table where a choice
has more than two branches, what changes **on each platform**, and the Storybook pages for the
components the page is about. A page leaves out the parts it has nothing for.

## Sources

Where a page quotes a number, it links where the number comes from.

- [Apple's Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/)
  for how a platform behaves on iOS and macOS: hit targets, safe areas, sheets and popovers.
- [GNOME's Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/) for desktop tools: design
  for people's goals, avoid confirmations and offer undo, keep the interface quiet.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) for the numbers that are law somewhere: contrast,
  target size, visible focus.
