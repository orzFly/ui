> **Every action works by keyboard, by touch and through a screen reader, and every word and edge is
> readable in every theme.**

The bar is [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) level AA. Each number below links the success
criterion (SC) it comes from; where a page asks for more than AA, it says so and names the source.
@orz/ui's components are built on Radix, which brings roles, keyboard handling and focus management
with it. What they cannot do for an app is choose its words, its colours and what it puts on a
screen, and most of this page is about that.

## Contrast

<Example of={ContrastPairs} />

The table measures the theme you are reading this in; pick another from the theme menu and it
follows.

- **Text: 4.5:1** against what it sits on
  ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum)). Large text, 24px or 18.66px bold
  and up, needs 3:1.
- **Edges and states: 3:1** — the edge that shows where a field is, the focus ring, the thumb of a
  switch, an icon that carries meaning on its own
  ([SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast)).
- **Disabled controls are exempt.** They are drawn at half opacity on purpose; do not use the same
  look for anything a reader is meant to read.
- **Colour reads from the tokens, never a hard-coded value.** A token is held to these numbers in
  every theme; a hex code is held to them in the one theme it was picked in.
- **Never colour alone** ([SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color)). An invalid field
  is red *and* says what is wrong; a diff line is green *and* starts with `+`; a failed run is red
  *and* has a failed icon.

A row that fails is a gap in that palette, not a licence to copy it. A custom theme is held to the
same numbers, and the theme editor shows each token's ratio against the surface it is drawn on.

## Focus

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<div><Button variant="outline" className="border-ring ring-3 ring-ring/50">Save changes</Button></div>}>
    Keep the focus ring every @orz/ui control draws on `:focus-visible`, and give anything else that
    takes focus the same one.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<div><Button variant="outline" className="focus-visible:ring-0 focus-visible:border-border">Save changes</Button></div>}>
    Remove the ring because it looks heavy after a click. `:focus-visible` already leaves it off for
    a mouse click and shows it for the keyboard.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

- **Visible** ([SC 2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible)): whatever has keyboard focus
  shows it. A clickable surface that is not an @orz/ui control is a `<button>` or `<a>`, so it
  takes focus at all, and borrows the `focus-visible:` classes from `Button`.
- **Not hidden** ([SC 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum), new in 2.2):
  a sticky header, a toolbar or the on-screen keyboard never covers the focused element entirely.
  Give a scroller with a sticky header `scroll-padding-top` of the header's height, so Tab lands
  clear of it; `useScrollInsets` does the same for the page's own `scrollIntoView` calls, and
  counts the on-screen keyboard.
- **In reading order** ([SC 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-order)): Tab follows the
  DOM, so the DOM follows the layout. No positive `tabIndex`.
- **Returned.** A `Dialog`, `Sheet` or menu holds focus while open and gives it back to what opened
  it on close. When that opener is gone — a menu item that removed its own row — send focus to the
  next meaningful control yourself, as `LoadFailure`'s `retryRef` exists to.

## Keyboard

Everything a pointer can do, the keyboard can do
([SC 2.1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard)), and nothing the keyboard enters it cannot
leave ([SC 2.1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap)). The components follow the WAI-ARIA
patterns, so an app keeps to these keys and adds no competing ones:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| Tab, Shift+Tab | Moves between controls, and into and out of a group as one stop |
| Arrow keys | Move within a group: `RadioGroup`, `Tabs`, a menu, `Select`'s list, a `Slider` |
| Enter | Activates a button or link, submits a form, picks a menu item |
| Space | Activates a button, toggles a `Checkbox` or `Switch` |
| Escape | Closes the innermost open overlay, and nothing else |
| `/` | Focuses the page's search (`useSlashShortcut`); typed as itself inside a field |

**A shortcut that is one character can be turned off**
([SC 2.1.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#character-key-shortcuts)): speech input types stray single
keys. An app that binds `/` or any other single key offers a setting for keyboard shortcuts, and
does not mount the component that binds them while it is off.

## Target size

- **24 × 24px at least** for anything clickable
  ([SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum), AA, new in 2.2). A smaller target
  passes only when a 24px circle on its centre touches no other target.
- **44 × 44px on touch**, the size Apple's
  [Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
  ask of every control and [SC 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced) (AAA)
  asks of every target.
- **Drags have a click.** Anything done by dragging can also be done with single clicks or taps
  ([SC 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dragging-movements), AA, new in 2.2): a `Slider` moves
  to where its track is clicked, and with the arrow keys.

On a mouse, @orz/ui's `Button` is 32px tall and its `xs` size 24px; a `Checkbox` and a
`RadioGroup` item are 16px, and a `Switch` 20px tall. Those three pass by the spacing exception, so
keep other targets out of the 24px around them, and pair each with its `Label`: the words then
toggle it too. On a touch screen the controls grow; the **Pointer** and **Touch** buttons under any
example on this site show both.

## Names and roles

<Example of={NamedIconButtons} />

- **Every control has a name** a screen reader can announce
  ([SC 4.1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value)). A field gets a `Label` with
  `htmlFor`; an icon-only button gets an `aria-label`, and the same words in a `Tooltip` for sighted
  readers. A placeholder is not a name: it is gone once the reader types.
- **The visible words start the name** ([SC 2.5.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#label-in-name)): a
  button that shows "Save" is not named "Submit form", or a speech user saying "click Save" misses
  it.
- **Status is announced** ([SC 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#status-messages)) without moving
  focus. `toast()` goes through the `Toaster`'s live region, and `LoadFailure` is a
  `role="status"`; a message of your own that appears after an action needs one of the two.
- **An error says what to fix** ([SC 3.3.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#error-identification)), in
  text beside the field, and the field gets `aria-invalid`, which @orz/ui's inputs draw in red.
- **Structure is markup.** Headings are `h1`–`h3` in order, lists are lists, and a table of data is
  a `Table`, so a screen reader's headings and table navigation work.

## Motion and zoom

- **Motion of your own stops on request.** An animation that slides, zooms or parallaxes is turned
  off under `prefers-reduced-motion` (Tailwind's `motion-reduce:`); a fade may stay
  ([SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#animation-from-interactions), AAA, and Apple's HIG).
- **Zoom is the reader's.** Never set `maximum-scale` or `user-scalable=no` in the viewport tag.
  Text reaches 200% without loss ([SC 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#resize-text)) and a page
  works 320px wide without scrolling sideways
  ([SC 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#reflow)); a log, a diff or a table may scroll inside
  its own box.

## On each platform

| | Pointer and keyboard | Touch and screen reader |
|---|---|---|
| Moving around | Tab and the arrow keys, with the focus ring showing where | VoiceOver or TalkBack swipes through the page in DOM order |
| Hover | Tooltips appear on hover and on focus | There is no hover; a tooltip's words must also be the control's name |
| Target | 24px, or the spacing around it | 44px |
| Shortcuts | `/` and the menus' own keys | None; every shortcut has a visible control too |

## Checking a screen

1. Put the mouse away and do the screen's main task with the keyboard alone, watching the focus
   ring.
2. Go through the built-in themes, light and dark, and read every word; the contrast table above
   follows each one.
3. Zoom to 200%, then narrow the window to 320px.
4. Turn on VoiceOver or TalkBack and find every control by its name.
5. On a phone, or with **Touch** pressed under an example, reach every control with a thumb.
