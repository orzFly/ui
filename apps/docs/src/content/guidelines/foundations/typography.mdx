**Set the interface in `text-ui`, step down to `text-ui-sm` for what is secondary and to
`text-ui-xs` only for a count or a badge, and up to `text-title` for a title.** Hierarchy comes
from size, weight and the muted colour together, never from a fifth size.

## The scale

| Role | Classes | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Body, labels, menu items, button text | `text-ui` | {font("text-ui")}px |
| A title: a dialog, a sheet or a page's panel | `font-heading text-title font-medium` | {font("text-title")}px |
| Secondary: a timestamp, a hint, table text, a module's name in its header | `text-ui-sm text-muted-foreground` | {font("text-ui-sm")}px |
| A count or a badge, and nothing else | `text-ui-xs` | {font("text-ui-xs")}px |
| What a user copies or compares: ids, hashes, paths, code | `font-mono` at the size around it, or `text-ui-sm` inline | — |

A page heading in a document-like view can go larger (`text-2xl`, `text-3xl`), as these docs do;
inside a tool, a panel's title is `text-title`. The components follow the same scale: `DialogTitle`
and `SheetTitle` are `font-heading text-title font-medium`, a `CardTitle` is a module's name in
its ruled header, `font-heading text-ui-sm font-bold`, and a `Badge` is `text-ui-xs`.

## Family

`--font-ui` is Noto Sans and `--font-mono` Noto Sans Mono, then the system's faces. `styles.css`
leaves the fonts out so an app with its own pays nothing for them; `@orz/ui/fonts.css` loads Noto
Sans and Noto Sans Mono for Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and Vietnamese, and Noto Sans for Devanagari
too. A browser fetches a script's file only when a page draws its characters. Chinese, Japanese and
Korean come from the page's language: `lang="zh-Hans"` names Noto Sans SC, then PingFang SC and
Microsoft YaHei, `ja` Noto Sans JP, then Hiragino Sans and Yu Gothic, and so on. None of those is
shipped, so the system's, or one your app loads, draws them; one family for every language would
draw Chinese with Japanese glyph shapes. `font-sans` and `--font-heading` are the same family;
override `--font-heading` only if your app has a display face.

## Weight

`font-medium` marks a title or a label; `font-semibold` is for a page heading. Body text stays
regular. Bold inside a paragraph is for the one phrase a skimming reader must not miss, at most once
or twice per screen.

## Numbers

Numbers that sit in a column, tick in place, or are compared across rows take `tabular-nums`, so
every digit is the same width and a column of sizes lines up on the decimal point. The base layer
already gives every `<time>` element `tabular-nums`; a date or time you render belongs in one.

## Line length and truncation

Prose sits in at most about 75 characters a line
([Baymard Institute](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)), with `max-w-prose`, or
`max-w-3xl` for a page.
A single-line label that may be long takes `truncate` (or `min-w-0` on the flex child that holds it,
or it never shrinks); the full text stays reachable somewhere other than a hover, because a
`title` tooltip does not exist on a touchscreen. A long unbroken string in a block, such as a URL or
a hash, takes `break-words` so it cannot push the layout sideways.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** Inputs are `md:text-ui`, the body size.
- **Touch.** An `Input`, `Textarea` and `HighlightedInput` are `text-base` below `md`, and on a
  coarse pointer at every width, because a tablet is wider than `md`. iOS Safari zooms the page into
  any field whose text is under 16px when it takes focus
  ([how Safari decides](https://thingsthemselves.com/no-input-zoom-in-safari-on-iphone-the-pixel-perfect-way/)),
  and does not zoom back out. Keep that in any field you build: `text-base md:text-ui touch:text-base`.
- **Both.** Sizes are in `rem`, so a reader who raises the browser's text size gets larger text
  everywhere. Text must survive 200% without loss
  ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)); do not set a fixed
  height on a box that holds text.
