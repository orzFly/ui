screen out for a phone first, then add `md:` for the rest.**

<Example of={MailToolbar} />

Below {minWidth(MD_UP)}px the search takes the whole width, and the label filter and **Compose** share the row
under it; from `md` up, all three sit on one line. One tree, laid out by CSS.

## Spacing

The more two things belong together, the closer they sit. A handful of steps cover nearly
everything:

| Between | Gap | |
|---|---|---|
| An icon and its label, inside a control | `gap-1.5` | 6px |
| Controls that act together: a toolbar, a field and its button | `gap-2` | 8px |
| Modules: between two, and around the region they fill | `ModuleGrid` | {gap(classes.moduleGrid)}px |
| The rows of a form, the items of a list with padding of their own | `gap-4` | 16px |
| Groups: a form's sections, a card's header and body | `gap-6` | 24px |
| Sections of a page | `gap-8` or more | 32px+ |

Use `gap` on the parent rather than margins on the children: the space then belongs to the layout,
and a child dropped in or taken out never leaves a double gap behind. Padding inside a panel
matches the gap between its groups, so a card is `p-4` with `gap-4` inside.

<Example of={AccountSections} />

**Profile** and **Access tokens** are sections, `gap-8` apart. Inside each, the heading, the body
and the buttons are groups, `gap-6`; the form's rows and the tokens are `gap-4`; the two buttons
that close the form act together, `gap-2`. The icon in **Compose** above sits `gap-1.5` from its
label, which `Button` does by itself.

A long run of rows alike, a mailbox or a group of settings, is where space is not enough: each row
holds several parts, and a hairline between the rows says which parts go together. A list's rows
take it from `List`; a group of settings puts a `Separator` with `purpose="row"` between its rows,
which a style whose dialogs part rows by space alone leaves blank.

Groups that are not rows of one list take a `Separator` when space is not enough between them:
clusters of buttons in a toolbar, where a gap wide enough to set them apart would scatter the row.
It is an element of the layout, so the parent's `gap` spaces it like any other child, and
`orientation="vertical"` stands it between the items of a row. A pane's own edge, such as a
sidebar's, is that `Pane`'s `edge` instead: the line belongs to the pane and goes with it, and
`edge={{ md: "end" }}` draws it only from the breakpoint where the panes stand side by side.

<Example of={FormattingToolbar} />

The toolbar's buttons fall into three groups: how text looks, how a block is shaped, what is
attached. A separator between groups in a `role="toolbar"` takes `decorative={false}`, so a screen
reader hears where one group ends; elsewhere it stays decorative, as it is by default.

## Modules

**A page of modules lays them out in a `ModuleGrid`, which stands them {gap(classes.moduleGrid)}px apart and pads the
region by the same amount; the page writes no spacing of its own between modules.** Each `Card`
keeps its own rule on every side, and the page's ground shows between them.

<Example of={ModuleRegion} />

Columns and spans go in the grid's `className` (`md:grid-cols-4`, `col-span-full`). An aside of
stacked modules is a `ModuleGrid` inside the grid: a grid inside another adds no padding of its
own, so the aside's edges line up with its neighbours'. Modules in one row match heights; a stack
keeps its modules at their own.

A reader that fills the height beside a rail gives the grid one row, `grid-rows-[minmax(0,1fr)]`,
and lets the reader's body (`min-h-0 overflow-y-auto`) and the rail scroll on their own.

Figures that read as one, such as the cells of a single stat strip, are cells ruled off inside one
`Card`, never cards set edge to edge. A `Pane` is the app's frame rather than a module: panes meet
along their edges, and a grid of modules sits inside one.

## Breakpoints

Tailwind's breakpoints are the library's, and the line that matters is `md`, {minWidth(MD_UP)}px: below it is a
phone, at or above it is everything else.

| Prefix | From | Use |
|---|---|---|
| — | 0 | The phone layout: one column, full-width fields, navigation in a `Sheet` |
| `sm:` | {minWidth(SM_UP)}px | Small adjustments: a dialog's buttons side by side |
| `md:` | {minWidth(MD_UP)}px | The desktop layout: side-by-side panes, a sidebar, a toolbar on one line |
| `lg:`, `xl:` | 1024px, 1280px | Extra panes that only fit on a wide screen, such as a contents column |

A layout change is CSS (`md:flex-row`) wherever the same elements can be rearranged. Branch in
JavaScript only when the tree itself differs, such as a pane that becomes a pushed page, and then
use the library's own query so CSS and script never disagree on where the line is:

```tsx
import { MD_UP, useMediaQuery } from "@orz/ui/hooks/use-media-query";

const wide = useMediaQuery(MD_UP);
```

<Example of={MailPanes} />

Narrow the window to walk the table. From `lg` up the folders sit in a pane of their own; below it
they move into a `Sheet` behind **Folders**. From `md` up the conversation opens beside the list;
below it, it replaces the list and the arrow goes back. Every change is a `max-md:` or `lg:` class,
so the tree stays the same. A dialog's footer puts its buttons side by side from `sm` up by itself;
[Overlays](/guidelines/patterns/overlays/) shows one.

**Width decides the layout; the pointer decides the room around the controls.** A tablet is wide
and touched, a narrow desktop window is small and clicked. Do not size controls by width, and do
not lay out by pointer: the components already make room on a coarse pointer by themselves.

## Width

- Content reflows down to 320px wide with no sideways scrolling
  ([WCAG 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)); only something
  two-dimensional by nature, a table, a log, a diff, scrolls inside its own box.
- A row that holds a long label gives that label `min-w-0 flex-1` and `truncate`, so the label
  shrinks and its neighbours keep their size.
- The window's frame meets its edges: the header, a sidebar and a toolbar run to the window's sides,
  and a tool's panes fill the window. A filled pane never sits inside a centred box, where its fill
  would stop short of the edge and float.
- Prose stops at a readable measure (`max-w-prose`, `max-w-3xl`) and centres in the window. A
  sidebar pushes it aside only when the window is too narrow to centre it clear of the sidebar. A
  page's contents sit right against the text.
- Pages that read alike keep the text in one place: a page without a sidebar leaves the sidebar's
  room free all the same, so its text starts where a page with one starts.

## Corners

`--radius` is the theme's corner, {radius}px by default, and the `rounded-*` steps scale from it:
`rounded-lg` is `--radius` itself, `rounded-md` and `rounded-sm` are smaller, `rounded-xl` and up
are larger. Use the steps rather than pixel values, so a theme that squares its corners squares
yours too. A box inside a rounded box takes a smaller step than its parent, so the two curves stay
concentric.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** Panes sit side by side and the page pads `px-10` or so at the edges.
- **Touch.** A phone pads `px-4` to `px-5`, enough to keep content off the screen's edge and off
  the system's back gesture. Keep content out of the notch and the home indicator with the safe-area
  insets; [Touch & mobile](/guidelines/platforms/touch-and-mobile/#safe-areas) covers them.
