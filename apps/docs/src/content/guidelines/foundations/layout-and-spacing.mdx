## Spacing

The more two things belong together, the closer they sit. A handful of steps cover nearly
everything:

| Between | Gap | |
|---|---|---|
| An icon and its label, inside a control | `gap-1.5` | 6px |
| Controls that act together: a toolbar, a field and its button | `gap-2` | 8px |
| The rows of a form, the items of a list with padding of their own | `gap-4` | 16px |
| Groups: a form's sections, a card's header and body | `gap-6` | 24px |
| Sections of a page | `gap-8` or more | 32px+ |

Use `gap` on the parent rather than margins on the children: the space then belongs to the layout,
and a child dropped in or taken out never leaves a double gap behind. Padding inside a panel
matches the gap between its groups, so a card is `p-4` with `gap-4` inside.

<Example of={AccountSections} />

**Profile** and **Access tokens** are sections, `gap-8` apart. Inside each, the heading, the body
and the buttons are groups, `gap-6`; the form's rows and the tokens are `gap-4`; the two buttons
that close the form act together, `gap-2`. The icon in **Compose** above sits `gap-1.5` from its
label, which `Button` does by itself.

A long run of rows alike, a mailbox or a group of settings, is where space is not enough: each row
holds several parts, and a hairline `divide-y` between the rows says which parts go together.

## Breakpoints

Tailwind's breakpoints are the library's, and the line that matters is `md`, 768px: below it is a
phone, at or above it is everything else.

| Prefix | From | Use |
|---|---|---|
| — | 0 | The phone layout: one column, full-width fields, navigation in a `Sheet` |
| `sm:` | 640px | Small adjustments: a dialog's buttons side by side |
| `md:` | 768px | The desktop layout: side-by-side panes, a sidebar, a toolbar on one line |
| `lg:`, `xl:` | 1024px, 1280px | Extra panes that only fit on a wide screen, such as a contents column |

A layout change is CSS (`md:flex-row`) wherever the same elements can be rearranged. Branch in
JavaScript only when the tree itself differs, such as a pane that becomes a pushed page, and then
use the library's own query so CSS and script never disagree on where the line is:

```tsx
import { MD_UP, useMediaQuery } from "@orz/ui/hooks/use-media-query";

const wide = useMediaQuery(MD_UP);
```

<Example of={MailPanes} />

Narrow the window to walk the table. From `lg` up the folders sit in a pane of their own; below it
they move into a `Sheet` behind **Folders**. From `md` up the conversation opens beside the list;
below it, it replaces the list and the arrow goes back. Every change is a `max-md:` or `lg:` class,
so the tree stays the same. A dialog's footer puts its buttons side by side from `sm` up by itself;
[Overlays](/guidelines/patterns/overlays/) shows one.

**Width decides the layout; the pointer decides the size.** A tablet is wide and touched, a narrow
desktop window is small and clicked. Do not size controls by width, and do not lay out by pointer:
the components already grow on a coarse pointer by themselves.

## Width

- Content reflows down to 320px wide with no sideways scrolling
  ([WCAG 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)); only something
  two-dimensional by nature, a table, a log, a diff, scrolls inside its own box.
- A row that holds a long label gives that label `min-w-0 flex-1` and `truncate`, so the label
  shrinks and its neighbours keep their size.
- Prose stops at a readable measure (`max-w-prose`, `max-w-3xl`); a tool's panes fill the window.

## Corners

`--radius` is the theme's corner, 10px by default, and the `rounded-*` steps scale from it:
`rounded-lg` is `--radius` itself, `rounded-md` and `rounded-sm` are smaller, `rounded-xl` and up
are larger. Use the steps rather than pixel values, so a theme that squares its corners squares
yours too. A box inside a rounded box takes a smaller step than its parent, so the two curves stay
concentric.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** Panes sit side by side and the page pads `px-10` or so at the edges.
- **Touch.** A phone pads `px-4` to `px-5`, enough to keep content off the screen's edge and off
  the system's back gesture. Keep content out of the notch and the home indicator with the safe-area
  insets; [Touch & mobile](/guidelines/platforms/touch-and-mobile/#safe-areas) covers them.
