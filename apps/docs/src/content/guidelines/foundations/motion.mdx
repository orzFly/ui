**Animate only to show where something came from or went, keep it under 200ms, and leave it out for
anyone who asked the system for less motion.** In a tool used all day, motion that does not explain
anything is waiting time.

<Example of={ArrivingRows} />

Each new step fades and drops in from just above in 150ms, so the eye finds the row that changed.
The drop carries `motion-safe:`, so with reduced motion turned on the row fades in where it stands.

## What moves, and how long

The components already animate, and an app adds little to them:

| What | Motion | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| A menu, popover, select, tooltip or hover card | Fades in, zooms from 95% and slides 8px out of its trigger | 100ms |
| A dialog and its overlay | Fades and zooms in place | 100ms |
| A sheet | Slides in from its edge | 200ms |
| An auto-hiding header, once scrolling stops | Settles fully in or out | 150ms |
| A block a link jumps to (`revealBlock`) | A tint that fades out | 2s |
| A `Skeleton` | Pulses until the content arrives | until then |

Motion of your own stays in the same range: 100 to 150ms for something small appearing, up to 200 to
250ms for something that crosses the screen. Something entering eases out (fast, then settling);
something leaving eases in. Leaving is quicker than entering, because nobody needs to watch a
thing go.

## When not to

- **Not while a finger drives it.** A sheet dragged by a thumb, a header pulled by a scroll, a
  slider: the element follows the finger with no transition, and animates only to settle once the
  finger lets go. A transition there trails the finger and feels broken.
- **Not on what the user is reading.** A list that re-sorts, a log that appends, a value that
  updates in place: change it, do not slide it. Motion draws the eye, and the eye was busy.
- **Not to fill a wait.** A spinner or skeleton says something is coming; it does not make it
  arrive sooner. A result that comes back within a few hundred milliseconds needs no loading state
  at all.
- **Not in a loop.** Anything that moves on its own for more than five seconds needs a way to stop
  it ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)); a
  `Skeleton` stops by being replaced.

<DoDont>
  <Do>
    Put the movement behind `motion-safe:`, as in `animate-in fade-in motion-safe:slide-in-from-top-1`,
    so only people who have not asked for less see it.
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    Animate layout (height, width, position of large areas) or loop an animation for attention. It
    costs frames on a phone and makes some readers dizzy.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Reduced motion

People who get motion sick, or who find movement distracting, turn on **Reduce motion** in their
system settings, and the browser reports it as `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html);
[Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)).
Tailwind's `motion-safe:` applies a class only without it, and `motion-reduce:` only with it.

- Put movement (slides, zooms, spins) behind `motion-safe:`.
- A fade is not motion in this sense and can stay; a reduced variant replaces a slide with a fade
  rather than with nothing, so the change is still noticed.
- `@orz/ui`'s own overlays do not read the setting yet: menus, dialogs and sheets zoom and slide
  regardless.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** Hover feedback is a colour change with `transition-colors`, never a movement.
- **Touch.** A press shows at once, with no transition to wait for, and nothing moves under the
  finger before it lifts.
