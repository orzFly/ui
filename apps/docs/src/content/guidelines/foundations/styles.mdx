## What a style changes

A style is the tokens `@orz/ui`'s components read for everything but colour, and a few rules for
what a token cannot draw:

- **Shape.** Corner radii, and how an edge is drawn: a flat 1px rule, or the two-tone bevel a
  Windows 2000 button has.
- **Depth.** Whether surfaces cast shadows, and what lifts a menu or a dialog above the page.
- **Type.** The families, the size of body text and each step of the scale.
- **Density at a pointer.** The height of a control and of a row, and the padding inside them.
- **Focus.** How the focus indicator is drawn: a solid ring, or a dotted rectangle.
- **State.** How a selected row, a highlighted menu item and the current tab are told apart.
- **Motion.** How long an overlay takes to arrive, and whether it fades, zooms or slides.
- **Its own colours.** A style may ask its themes for colour tokens beyond the shared ones, such as
  raymond's 3D face and edges and its title bar's gradient.

## What a style never changes

These hold under every style, and outrank it:

- The components' structure, props and behaviour. Code written against one style runs under all of
  them.
- The sizes on a coarse pointer: 40px and 44px controls, a 44px hit area around anything drawn
  smaller, and 16px text in a field, so a phone does not zoom. A style sizes for the mouse only
  ([Density](/guidelines/foundations/density/)).
- The contrast floors: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for an edge a control is found by and for a state cue.
  Every palette a style ships is held to them
  ([Accessibility](/guidelines/content/accessibility/#contrast)).
- A visible focus indicator, and reduced motion
  ([Motion](/guidelines/foundations/motion/#reduced-motion)).

## Offering styles

A theme belongs to the styles it lists in `styles`, or to the default style when it lists none, so
the user picks a style and then one of its themes.

- **Offer the styles whose stylesheets you import.** `createThemeStore({ styles, defaultStyle })`
  takes them and starts a new user on `defaultStyle`; without `styles` the store offers the default
  style alone, and the menu has no Style row. Beside the default style, `@orz/ui` ships raymond,
  Windows 2000: import `@orz/ui/styles/raymond.css` after `@orz/ui/styles.css`, and offer
  `raymondStyle` from `@orz/themes`, whose built-in themes include its own.
- **Let the user switch.** With more than one style, `ThemeMenu` shows a Style row above the modes
  and previews a style while it is hovered; from code, `setStyle` on the store or from `useStyle()`
  switches it. Either way the user lands on the theme they last picked under that style, or on its
  first theme.
- **Hold the user to one style** by offering that one alone. A terminal theme is not tied to a
  style: terminal output is content, and its own pick shows under any.

## Your own surfaces

<DoDont>
  <Do>
    Build a panel from `Card`, a toolbar from `Button`, a list's rows at `min-h-row`, and round a
    part of your own with `rounded-control` or `rounded-surface`. Under another style it changes
    with everything around it.
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    Write `rounded-lg`, `h-8` or `shadow-md` on a part of your own, or style by
    `[data-style="raymond"]`. The first stays as it is when the style changes; the second breaks
    under every style you did not write it for.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

The numbers the other guideline pages give, a row's height or a button's, are the default style's.
Another style draws the same parts at its own sizes, and the pages' rules hold for it all the same.
