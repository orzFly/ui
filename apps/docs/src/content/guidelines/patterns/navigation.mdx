**Build navigation from these three and leave their look alone. A hand-made row or tab draws its
own current state, and an app's places then read differently from screen to screen.**

<Example of={AppFrame} />

The header's `TabNav` holds the app's sections; the sidebar's `NavList` holds the places inside
the one that is open, under a heading for each group. Pick a section or a folder: the current one
is marked the same way in both, by weight, with an underline on the header's rule or a tick on the
row's leading edge.

## Which one

| You need to | Use |
|---|---|
| Go between an app's few top-level sections, from its header | `TabNav`, with a `TabNavLink` for each |
| Go between many places: folders, settings sections, files, a document's parts | `NavList`, a `NavListLink` for each, `NavListHeading` over each group |
| Show one of a few views of the same thing, in place | `Tabs` |
| The same, in a bar over a pane that has a surface of its own | `Tabs` with `TabsList variant="line"` |

`Tabs` switch panels and keep the page's URL; `TabNav` and `NavList` go somewhere, and each place
is a link wherever it has a URL. Pass a router's link with `asChild`, or a `button` where the
place is app state rather than an address.

<Example of={LineTabs} />

The default tabs draw the current one as a box joined to its panel below. Where the panel does
not lie right under the tabs, as in a bar over a demo with its own sidebar, the box would hang
over something else: use the line variant, laid flush on the bar's bottom rule.

## Marking where the reader is

- Mark exactly one place with `current`. It sets `aria-current` and draws the state; a row or a tab
  never adds a fill or a bar of its own.
- `current` is `"page"` by default. A part of a document in view is `current="location"`.
- A section in `TabNav` stays current on every page inside it, not only on its own first page.
- Nest places with `depth`, one step per level, rather than wrapping a list in an indented box.

## On each platform

### Desktop & pointer

Rows run at the dense row height, with the count, size or offset at the row's end in tabular
figures (`NavListCount`).

### Touch & mobile

Under a coarse pointer every row and tab grows to a touch target by itself. A `TabNav` has no room
on a phone: put its sections in a `Sheet` opened from the header, as a `NavList` with the open
section's places nested under it, as this site does.
