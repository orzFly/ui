**Build navigation from these and leave their look alone. A hand-made row or tab draws its
own current state, and an app's places then read differently from screen to screen.**

<Example of={AppFrame} />

The header's `TabNav` holds the app's sections; the sidebar's `NavList` holds the places inside
the one that is open, under a heading for each group. Pick a section or a folder: the current one
is marked the same way in both, by weight, with an underline on the header's rule or a tick on the
row's leading edge.

## Which one

| You need to | Use |
|---|---|
| Go between an app's few top-level sections, from its header | `TabNav`, with a `TabNavLink` for each |
| Go between many places: folders, settings sections, files, a document's parts | `NavList`, a `NavListLink` for each, `NavListHeading` over each group |
| Let the reader close a group of places they seldom need | `NavListGroup`, headed by a `NavListHeading` holding a `NavListGroupToggle`, or by a `NavListGroupRow` when the group has a page of its own |
| Go between places in a hierarchy the reader opens and closes: folders, machines, a file's parts | `Tree`, a `TreeItem` for each place |
| Go between a repository's files and folders, by path | `FileTree`, a `FileTreeItem` for each |
| Go to a section of the page in view, from a column beside it | `NavList variant="rail"` |
| Send the reader to another page from inside the prose, saying what is there | `LinkCard`, with a `LinkCardTitle` and a `LinkCardDescription`; `variant="row"` for a list of pages |
| Show one of a few views of the same thing, in place | `Tabs` |
| The same, in a bar over a pane that has a surface of its own | `Tabs` with `TabsList variant="line"` |
| The same, framed with its panels as one module, other controls beside the tabs | `Tabs variant="framed"`, the strip on a `TabsBar` |

A `NavList` that is a pane of the app's frame, as a mail client's folders beside its messages are,
sits in a `NavListFrame` around its `nav`, its headings and its scroller, which a style may draw as
a well. One in a `Sheet` does not: the sheet is its frame.

`Tabs` switch panels and keep the page's URL; `TabNav` and `NavList` go somewhere, and each place
is a link to its URL, so a middle click, a modified click and the context menu all work as they
do on any link ([A place is a link](/guidelines/principles/#a-place-is-a-link)). Pass a router's
link with `asChild`; an app that moves in place by hand keeps the `href` and returns early from
its click handler unless `isPlainClick(event)` from `@orz/ui/lib/link`. The same holds off these
components: a row that opens an item, a **Back** to the list, a **Clear search** that returns to
a folder, each is a link, drawn by `Button asChild` or `ListItem asChild` around the `a`.

<Example of={LineTabs} />

The default tabs draw the current one as a box joined to its panel below. Where the panel does
not lie right under the tabs, as in a bar over a demo with its own sidebar, the box would hang
over something else: use the line variant, laid flush on the bar's bottom rule.

## Groups that open and close

<Example of={CollapsibleGroups} />

When a column holds many groups and the reader works in one or two of them, let the groups close
in a `NavListGroup`. A few groups of a few places each stay open: closing them only costs the
reader clicks. A group with no page of its own makes its `NavListHeading` the toggle. A group that
is a place heads itself with a `NavListGroupRow`: its link goes to the place, and opens the group
too if it was closed, never closing it; the toggle at the row's end does the opening and closing.

A group the reader has not touched is open exactly while it holds the current place, from the
first frame. Once the reader closes it over the current place, its head is drawn as current, so
the reader still sees where they are. Groups hold no groups: deeper places, opened and closed as
the reader works, are a [`Tree`](#trees).

## Trees

<Example of={FolderTree} />

Places nested a level or two and always shown open are a `NavList`, each level a `depth` deeper;
one level the reader may close is a [group](#groups-that-open-and-close). When the reader opens and closes the branches, or the nesting runs as deep as the data does, they
are a `Tree`. A click on a place goes there, and opens it as well if it was closed; only the
toggle beside it, or ←, closes it, so going to a folder never hides what is in it.

Mark the place on screen with `current`: the tree opens the way to it, and the keyboard starts
there. An app that holds the open branches itself starts them from `ancestorsOf`, so the first
frame already shows the place. When a filter leaves no row, the tree draws nothing; show an
`EmptyState` in its place.

### Files

<Example of={RepositoryFiles} />

When the places are files known by their paths, as a repository's or a folder's are, use a
`FileTree`: it takes the paths as they come, puts folders first, merges a chain of folders that
hold one folder each into one row, and draws each file's type as its icon. Use a plain `Tree`
when the data is not paths, or when its folders load as the reader opens them.

Give it each file's status, and it marks the row: a letter at the end in the status's colour
(**A**dded, **M**odified, **D**eleted, **R**enamed, **U**ntracked), the name in that colour too
and struck through when deleted, a dot on a folder holding a change, and an ignored file or
folder faded, with everything under an ignored folder. The current file's name keeps the tree's
own colour, the place outranking its status; the letter still says it.

A filter by text can leave only the matches (`filterMode="hide"`, the default), for finding one
file. It can keep every row and open the folders on the way to each match (`"expand"`), to see the
matches among their neighbours, or open those folders alone and close the rest (`"collapse"`), to
see which parts of the tree the matches fall in. Under either of the last two the reader opens and
closes folders for the search alone, and clearing it brings back the folders as they were. Say
how many files match, and say it when none do.

## Marking where the reader is

- Mark exactly one place with `current`. It sets `aria-current` and draws the state; a row or a tab
  never adds a fill or a bar of its own.
- `current` is `"page"` by default. A part of a document in view is `current="location"`.
- A section in `TabNav` stays current on every page inside it, not only on its own first page.
- Nest places that stay open with `depth`, one step per level, rather than wrapping a list in an
  indented box; nest places the reader opens and closes in a `NavListGroup`, or a `Tree` when
  they run deeper than one level.

## On each platform

### Desktop & pointer

Rows run at the dense row height, with the count, size or offset at the row's end in tabular
figures (`NavListCount`).

### Touch & mobile

Under a coarse pointer every row and tab grows to a touch target by itself. A `TabNav` has no room
on a phone: put its sections in a `Sheet` opened from the header, as a `NavList` with each
section a `NavListGroup` of its places, open on the section the reader is in, as this site does.
