**Lay a form out as one column: a visible label above each field, the error or hint under it, and
one primary button at the end that names what it saves.**

<Example of={ProfileForm} />

Submit it with the email empty: the message lands under the field, the field is marked invalid,
and the rest of the form keeps what was typed.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<LabelAboveField />}>
    Give every field a `Label` that stays visible, joined through `htmlFor`. A placeholder can show
    an example value.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<PlaceholderAsLabel />}>
    Use the placeholder as the label: it disappears with the first keystroke, and with it what the
    field was for.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do>
    Check a field when the form is submitted, and again as it is corrected once it has been marked
    wrong.
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    Mark a field wrong while it is still being typed for the first time, or disable the submit
    button without saying what is missing.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Fields

| The value | Use |
|---|---|
| One line of text, a number, an email, a URL | `Input` with the matching `type` and `autoComplete` |
| Several lines of text | `Textarea` |
| One of a fixed list | `Select`, or a `RadioGroup` for two to five, see [Choosing a control](/guidelines/patterns/choosing-a-control/) |
| Yes or no, applied on submit | `Checkbox` |
| Text in a query language, with completion | `QueryBox` |

Mark the optional fields "(optional)" in the label rather than every required one with an asterisk:
most fields in a short form are required, and a reader should not have to decode a symbol.

## Errors

- The message goes in a paragraph under its field, the field gets `aria-invalid`, and
  `aria-describedby` points at the message, so a screen reader reads it with the field.
- After a failed submit, move focus to the first invalid field.
- Say how to fix it: "Enter an email address, like alice@example.com", not "Invalid input".
- An error from the server about the whole form, a lost connection or a conflict, sits above the
  buttons, where people look before pressing them again.

## Leaving with unsaved changes

A form that holds typed work guards it. When people navigate away before saving, ask with
`UnsavedChangesDialog`: **Keep editing** is its primary action, and **Discard and leave** is the
alternative. The dialog takes no router; the app's own navigation blocker opens it.

Settings that apply as they change (Switches, see [Choosing a control](/guidelines/patterns/choosing-a-control/))
have nothing unsaved, so a settings page of Switches needs no guard and no Save button.

## On each platform

### Desktop & pointer

- Enter in a single-line field submits the form; Tab moves through the fields in reading order.
- Two short related fields, a first and last name, may share a row on a wide screen. Everything else
  stays in one column.

### Touch

- The `type` and `inputMode` pick the on-screen keyboard: `email` gets an @ key, `numeric` a digit
  pad. `autoComplete` lets the phone fill the field in.
- Inputs use a 16px font on touch, so the phone does not zoom into the field on focus.
- The on-screen keyboard covers the lower half of the screen. Keep the submit button in the flow
  under the last field, not pinned to the bottom, where the keyboard hides it.
