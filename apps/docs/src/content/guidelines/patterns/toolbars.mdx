**A row of commands is a `Toolbar`. It decides what fits; you decide the order, what stays
together and what goes first. Never hide a command at a breakpoint by hand.**

<Example of={IssueToolbar} />

Drag the frame's corner. As the bar narrows, the search narrows, the filters drop their names,
the empty search gives way to its icon, and then the filters go into the `⋯` menu, the one at the
end first. **New issue** stays to the last. Set a filter away from its default and it leaves the
bar last; once it is in the `⋯`, the `⋯` counts it.

## What gives way, in order

Each step is taken only when the one before has not made enough room, and a wider bar takes them
back in reverse, so a width always gets the same layout however it was reached.

1. A `ToolbarSpacer` gives up its room, and a `ToolbarSearch` or a `flex` `ToolbarItem` narrows.
2. On a fine pointer, the words of commands with an icon hide, the lowest `priority` first, every
   command of a priority at once. The icon stays, and the tooltip says the words. A primary or
   destructive command keeps its words: what it does is said before it is done.
3. A `SegmentedControl` becomes a dropdown of its value, and an empty search becomes its icon.
4. Commands go into the `⋯` menu: the lowest `priority` first; within a priority, those at their
   defaults before those set away from them (a filter in use, a toggle that is on); with a
   `ToolbarSpacer`, the cluster before it before the one after it; each from the end of its
   cluster.
5. Commands with `priority="pin"` go last of all, and only those with a form in the menu. What is
   still too wide scrolls rather than being cut off.

The `⋯` stands at the end of the first cluster that overflowed, so a command at the far end, the
one a reader looks for there, keeps its place. Put it somewhere else with `ToolbarOverflow`.

None of this changes the bar's height. It is as tall as its tallest part in any of its forms, so
what lies below it stays put as a search gives way to its icon, a segmented control to its
dropdown, or a part to the `⋯`.

## What fits follows the style and the pointer

How many commands fit at a width is whatever their sizes in the style and on the pointer in use
allow: a style with smaller controls holds more, and a coarse pointer, whose commands keep room
beside them for a finger, holds fewer. So a toolbar states an order, groups and priorities, never
a breakpoint: a command hidden at a breakpoint follows the window rather than the bar, and stays
hidden when a denser style would have made room for it.

On a coarse pointer no words hide: a tooltip never shows under a finger, so an icon alone would
lose its name. A command that does not fit moves into the menu whole, words and all.

## Clusters and the ⋯

A `ToolbarSpacer` splits the bar into the commands at its start and those at its end, as Mail's
thread toolbar keeps **Star** at the far end. Commands that belong together go in a
`ToolbarGroup`, which moves into the menu whole and is parted from its neighbours there; a
`ToolbarSeparator` between commands parts them in the menu too. A separator left with nothing to
part, at an end or beside the `⋯`, is not drawn.

Commands that are always in the `⋯`, Mail's **Forward** and **Copy link**, are the children of a
`ToolbarOverflow`. Its `⋯` is always shown, and what the bar moves into it goes above them.

A [`SelectionBar`](/guidelines/patterns/actions-and-buttons/#acting-on-a-selection) is a toolbar
too: its select-all checkbox, the count and **Clear selection** never go into the `⋯`, and what
it holds between them gives way as here. Give it the parts themselves, never a `Toolbar` of their
own: a toolbar in another's row draws a second bar, a second Tab stop and a second `⋯`, and warns
in development.

## One of a few

<Example of={ViewAndAlignment} />

Both pick one of a few, and they give way differently.

- A `SegmentedControl` is a mode of the tool with words: a view, a typing mode. Short of room it
  first becomes a dropdown of its current value, then radio items in the `⋯` menu.
- A `ToolbarGroup` with `selection="single"` is a row of icon toggles: an alignment. It stays a row
  until it moves into the menu whole, as radio items under its `label`. With
  `selection="multiple"` each toggle is on or off on its own, bold and italic, and they move as
  check items.

## Menus, filters and other parts

A command whose menu should follow it into the `⋯` is a `ToolbarMenu`, which becomes a submenu
there, its `value` beside its name. A `FilterMenu` in a toolbar becomes a row naming its
`toolbarLabel` and `toolbarValue`; choosing the row opens the filter against the `⋯` and gives the
focus back to the `⋯` when it closes. While it is open it stays where it stood, on the bar or in the
`⋯`, however its picks change its width, so a `selection="multiple"` one opened from the `⋯` takes
several picks in a row and still closes back to the `⋯`. The parts before it still give way, and
move its trigger; its menu stays put while the pointer is on it or near it, and slides over once
the pointer has gone. A `SplitButton` becomes its command and a submenu of the
others, or the others after it with `overflow="group"`.

A `ToolbarButton` wrapped in a menu or popover trigger of your own cannot be followed into the
menu, since its surface would have nothing on the bar to open from: it stays on the bar, and warns
in development. Make it a `ToolbarMenu`, or a `ToolbarItem` with an `overflow` form.

Anything else is a `ToolbarItem`. Give it `overflow`, what it is in the `⋯` menu, or it stays on
the bar. That form is drawn in the menu, outside the item: it reads nothing from the providers
inside the item, so take what it needs from the code it is written in. A command moved into the
menu is called with the menu item's event, not its button's.

A title that takes the room the commands leave is a `ToolbarItem` with `flex` and a `minWidth`,
never text that grows or shrinks by itself: the bar cannot know how wide such text wants to be,
so it moves the wrong parts, and warns in development.

## Set away from its default

A filter in use changes what the rest of the page shows, so it is the part a reader most needs to
find again. A part set away from its default leaves the bar last within its priority. Once it is in
the `⋯`, the `⋯` counts it, and its row in the menu is marked: a dot before its value, or after its
name when it shows none, and the value in the row's own colour rather than the quiet one of the
rows at their defaults. A list that has been narrowed keeps the reason in sight.

Two kinds of part know it by themselves: a `ToolbarButton` whose `aria-pressed` is on, such as
Mail's **Star**, and a `ToolbarGroup` with `selection="multiple"` that has something chosen. Tell
the rest: `active` on a `ToolbarMenu`, a `ToolbarItem`, a `ToolbarButton` or a `ToolbarGroup`, and
`toolbarActive` on a `FilterMenu` or a `SegmentedControl`, which also stand outside toolbars, where
`active` would read as their chosen value. Pass it while the value differs from the one the page
starts with, as the example's filters do with `active={status !== "open"}`.

When given, `active` overrides what a part knows by itself: `active={false}` leaves a pressed
toggle out of the count, and `active` counts a `selection="single"` group that picks what a list
shows, while its choice is not the default one.

## Search

A `ToolbarSearch`'s field takes the bar's `size`, as its commands do, so on a fine pointer a bar
with a search is as tall as one without. On touch a field is taller, to hold its larger text, and
the bar keeps that height while the icon stands in for it.

A `ToolbarSearch` with a query never becomes its icon. Pressing the icon opens the field again at
its narrowest, moving other commands into the menu to make room; only when nothing else could make
room does the field take the whole bar, with a button back. The field stays one element through all
of this, so its caret and an input method's composition survive.

## The keyboard

A toolbar's commands are one Tab stop, and the arrow keys move between them, wrapping at the ends
and following the reading direction; a command in the menu is skipped. Tab comes back to the
command the focus last left. A search's field is a Tab stop of its own, so its arrow keys move its
caret, and Tab meets it where it stands, before the commands or after them.

## Before it is measured

A prerendered page draws every command first. What does not fit wraps onto a line that is cut off
rather than showing half a button, and the bar lays itself out once the page runs, which may move
it once. A `⋯` that only holds overflow appears then; one with commands of its own is there from
the start.
