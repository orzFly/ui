**Every row of a list ordered by hand has a drag handle and a pair of move buttons. Neither one
replaces the other.**

<Example of={NotificationChannels} />

Where a notification goes, tried from the top. Drag a row by its grip, or press its ↑ or ↓; with
the focus anywhere in a row, Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ (⌥ on a Mac) do what its buttons do. The other rows
make room while one is dragged, and slide to their new places after a press.

## When to order by hand

Give a list a hand-set order when the order means something only its owner knows: what to try
first, which rule matches before which, which way a fallback runs. A list that follows a field, a
date or a name, is sorted by that field, and its rows carry no handle.

## The handle and the buttons

The handle is for the pointer: it carries a row any distance in one gesture. The buttons are for
everyone else: the keyboard, a screen reader, a phone held in one hand, and anyone moving a row
by a place or two. Each button's tooltip names its key, so the keyboard shortcut is found where the
action already is.

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<HandleAndButtons />}>
    Give every row both: the grip at its start, the pair at its end.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<HandleOnly />}>
    Leave the buttons out. A drag is the only way to move these rows, which a keyboard, a screen
    reader and an unsteady hand cannot make
    ([WCAG 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)).
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

Do not repeat the buttons as **Move up** and **Move down** in the row's `⋯` menu: they are already
on the row. Where a row has a menu of its own, the pair goes just before it. A row that is a link
keeps the handle and the buttons beside the link, never inside it.

## Where a row may go

<Example of={RouteTable} />

Routes, matched from the top. The catch-all must stay last: it never moves, and no route may go
below it.

- A row that cannot move stays in the list, its handle and buttons disabled: `canMove`.
- A place that cannot take a row is refused before the drop, not undone after it: `canDrop`.
  A drag then holds the gap at the nearest place that takes the row, and a button steps past the
  refused places to the next one that does, or is disabled when there is none.
- When the rules are costly to ask or the list is long, answer for the buttons at once with
  `canMoveUp` and `canMoveDown`, rather than leaving `Reorder` to ask `canDrop` place by place.

`onMove` hears one move for each drag and each press: the row, where it was and where it went.
An app that keeps positions on a server sends that one change; one that keeps an array passes it
to `moveItem`, or `moveInGroups` for groups. `Reorder` changes nothing itself.

## Groups

<Example of={ChannelTiers} />

When the group a row is in is part of its order, as which channels are tried first, put every
group in one `Reorder` with `groups`, each group a `ReorderGroup` holding its heading and its rows.
A row then moves across: a drag carries it past a heading, and a button at a group's end steps into
the next group. Here **Notify first** takes at most two channels. An empty group shows a line
saying so, which a row dragged into it covers.

When the group is something else about the row, its owner or its state, give each group a
`Reorder` of its own: a row moves only within its group, and changing the group is an edit of the
row, from its menu or its form.

## Filtering and saving

- While a filter or a search hides some rows, draw no handles and no buttons: a place between rows
  the reader cannot see means nothing.
- Save a move as the page saves any other change: at once, or with the form's **Save**. Do not ask
  first, and do not toast it with **Undo**: moving the row back is the undo.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** Drag the handle; press the buttons, or Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ from anywhere in the row.
  The handle is no Tab stop: the keyboard has the buttons.
- **Touch.** A finger on the handle drags the row at once, with no long press; a finger anywhere
  else on the row scrolls the page. The buttons keep their size, take hit areas, and stand far
  enough apart that a thumb meant for one never presses the other.
