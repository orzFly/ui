**A surface has at most one primary button: the action most people came to take. Everything else
steps down a variant, and actions used now and then move into a menu.**

<Example of={ThreadActions} />

A conversation from the Mail demo. **Send** is the one primary button, and it waits until there is
a reply to send. Archive and Delete are ghost icon buttons, each with a name, and a tooltip that
also shows its key. Forward and Copy link are used now and then, so they wait behind **More
actions**. Archive or delete the conversation: the toolbar swaps in **Move to Inbox**, and the
toast takes it back.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<OnePrimaryAction />}>
    Make the action people came for the one primary button, and step the others down.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<CompetingPrimaryActions />}>
    Give every action the primary look: nothing stands out, so people read every label to find the
    one they want.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<NamedButtons />}>
    Label a button with a verb and its object, "Save profile", "Delete 3 files", so it still reads
    right out of context.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<VagueButtons />}>
    Label it "OK", "Submit" or "Yes": the button then only makes sense after reading the question
    above it.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Which variant

| The action | `variant` |
|---|---|
| The one action the surface exists for: Save, Publish, Send | `default` |
| A real alternative beside it: Cancel, Save draft, Back | `outline` or `secondary` |
| A repeated or toolbar action, usually an icon: Bold, Copy, Close | `ghost` |
| An action that deletes or discards | `destructive`, see [Destructive actions](/guidelines/patterns/destructive-actions/) |
| A navigation that looks like a link inside prose or a table | `link`, or a real `<a>` through `asChild` |

<Example of={ButtonVariants} />

Each row is taken from a demo: the Settings profile form's footer, the Mail toolbar, an access
token's row, and a failed run on the pull request's timeline, whose **Details** is a link.

The size follows the surface rather than the action: `default` in forms and dialogs, `sm` in dense
rows and table cells, and `lg` only for a call to action that stands alone.

An action that leaves the page is a link (`<Button asChild><a href>…</a></Button>`), so it opens in
a new tab and shows its address; an action that changes something is a button.

## Overflow

Put the actions a surface offers but people rarely take into a `DropdownMenu` behind a ghost icon
button named "More actions". Keep the order stable: frequent first, destructive last behind a
separator, each with `variant="destructive"`.

<Example of={TrashThreadMenu} />

A conversation in Mail's Trash. Open **More actions**: Forward and Copy link come first, and
**Delete forever…** waits last, after a separator, in the destructive colour. Its ellipsis says it
asks before it acts.

Do not hide the primary action in a menu, and do not put a single action in one: a menu with one
item is a button that costs two taps.

## On each platform

### Desktop & pointer

- An icon-only button has an `aria-label`, and a `Tooltip` that shows the same name on hover and on
  keyboard focus.
- A frequent action gets a keyboard shortcut, shown in its tooltip or menu item
  (`DropdownMenuShortcut`).
- Enter submits a form's primary action; Escape cancels a dialog, so neither needs a mouse.

### Touch

- Tooltips do not open on touch. The `aria-label` still names the button for a screen reader, but
  an icon whose meaning is not obvious needs a text label on a phone.
- Buttons take their touch sizes by themselves. Keep a row of them from wrapping into a second row
  that pushes content down: move the rare ones into the overflow menu instead.
- In a dialog footer on a narrow screen the buttons stack full width, the primary one on top, within
  reach of a thumb.
