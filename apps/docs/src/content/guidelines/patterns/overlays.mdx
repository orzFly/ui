**Pick the overlay that interrupts least. A Popover or a menu keeps the page in reach; a Sheet sets
it aside; a Dialog stops it until the question is answered.**

<Example of={OverlayKinds} />

Each control opens a different overlay. In Mail, the panel icon opens the folders in a `Sheet`, the
star is named by its `Tooltip`, and the tag icon opens the labels in a `DropdownMenu` that stays
open for the next tick. On the token, the pencil edits its name in a `Popover`, and **Revoke…** asks
in a `Dialog`. Hover **alice** for a `HoverCard`. Press **Touch** above the preview: the Tooltip
and the HoverCard no longer open, and every other control still works.

## Which one

| You need to | Use | Blocks the page | On a phone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask one question, or take one short input, before going on | `Dialog` | Yes | Centred, nearly full width; the footer buttons stack, primary on top |
| Show a panel of settings, filters or a navigation list beside the work | `Sheet` (`side="right"` or `"left"`) | Yes, dimmed | Three quarters of the width, from the edge |
| Offer a short list of actions or one choice from a few | `DropdownMenu` | Yes, until a pick or Escape | Opens under its trigger; rows grow to touch size |
| Edit one value in place, where the trigger is | `Popover` | No | Opens under its trigger, never wider than the screen |
| Preview what a link points to | `HoverCard` | No | Does not open: the link itself must be enough |
| Name an icon button | `Tooltip` | No | Does not open: the `aria-label` names it, the icon must be clear |

Open one overlay from another at most once: a menu or a Popover inside a dialog is fine. A dialog
that opens another dialog is a flow; make it a page, or one dialog with steps.

## Do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<QuestionDialog />}>
    Title a dialog with its question, "Delete … forever?", and put the action that answers it in
    the footer, named with the same verb: **Delete forever**.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<InfoDialog />}>
    Use a dialog to show information that needs no answer: a toast, an inline note or a Popover
    interrupts less.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do show={<PreviewOnHover />}>
    Put anything a HoverCard or Tooltip shows somewhere reachable without hover too: the page the
    link opens, or a visible label.
  </Do>
  <Dont show={<ActionInHoverCard />}>
    Hide an action inside a HoverCard: a phone cannot hover, and a keyboard reaches the card only
    through its trigger's focus.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Closing

Every overlay closes on Escape, and a Dialog, Sheet, Popover and menu on a click or tap outside
them. Focus returns to the trigger that opened them. A Dialog or Sheet also has its own close
button in the corner, so it can be closed with one tap on a phone, where there is no Escape key.

A click outside a Dialog that holds typed input should not throw the input away: keep it for the
next opening, or ask first. The click most likely meant to look at the page behind.

## On each platform

### Desktop & pointer

- A Popover, HoverCard or Tooltip opens beside its trigger and flips to the other side when there is
  no room; nothing needs placing by hand.
- A HoverCard opens after a short hover and stays open while the pointer is over the card, so its
  links can be clicked.
- Menus are fully keyboard-driven: arrows move, Enter picks, typing a letter jumps to an item.

### Touch

- Nothing opens on hover. A HoverCard and a Tooltip never appear, so neither may carry the only
  copy of anything.
- A Sheet is the phone's side panel: the site's own navigation on a phone is one. Prefer it over
  a Popover for anything longer than a field or two, where the on-screen keyboard would cover a
  Popover.
- A Dialog sits in the middle of the screen with the keyboard below it. Keep it short enough to fit
  above the keyboard: one or two fields.
