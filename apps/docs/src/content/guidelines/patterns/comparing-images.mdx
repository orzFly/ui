**When a picture changed, show its two versions as one `MediaCompare`, not as two images one
after the other. Both sides are drawn at one scale, so a picture that grew still looks bigger,
and the reader can lay one over the other to see exactly what moved.**

<Example of={SettingsRedesign} />

The settings screenshot gained a field and its Save button moved. Side by side shows both whole;
**Swipe** drags the newer over the older, **Onion skin** fades it in, and **Difference** blacks
out every pixel that did not change, so only what changed is left. Click either picture to open
the pair in the attachment viewer.

## Which mode

| The reader wants to | Mode |
|---|---|
| See both versions whole, or they differ in size or layout | Side by side |
| Find where a change begins and ends across the picture | Swipe |
| Judge a small shift, a nudged element or a recoloured one | Onion skin |
| Be sure nothing else changed | Difference |

The overlay modes line the two up at their top-left corner, as a screenshot is taken; a picture
drawn about its centre, as a diagram is, takes `align="center"`. They wait until both sides have
a size, and a side that failed to load leaves side by side the only mode.

## Labels and sizes

- Tag each side with what tells the versions apart: a version (`v1`, `v2`), a date, or
  Before and After when nothing else names them. A long tag is cut, and keeps its whole text as
  a tooltip.
- Each label shows the side's natural size, and marks a dimension the two disagree on. Scaling
  hides a size change, so the label is where the reader learns of it.
- `backdrop="checker"` shows transparency as the attachment viewer does, for screenshots and
  icons; `backdrop="page"` draws a picture on the page's own ground, for one made to sit on it.

## Several on one page

A page of comparisons is read one way: wrap them in one `CompareModeProvider`, and switching
any of them switches them all. Without it each keeps its own mode. Limit `modes` to what makes
sense for the pictures; a pair that can only sit side by side has no mode control at all.

## In the viewer

Detail a page cannot show is read in the attachment viewer: pass `onOpen`, and open the pair with
`openCompare` from `useAttachmentViewer`, or as `{ compare, index }` in a controlled viewer's
`state`. Hand it every comparison on the page, in reading order, so paging walks through them
as the page does. Both versions share one zoom and one position there, so a wheel or a drag over
either moves both, and the mode bar under them is the page's own when a `CompareModeProvider`
holds them all.

A side drawn as a node rather than an image file, such as a diagram's SVG, zooms as vectors, side
by side being its only mode. The two sit left and right, to be read across, unless the viewer is
as narrow as a page on which the comparison stacks; there they stack too.

## Pairs an author writes

In Markdown, an author compares two images by giving them a paragraph of their own with an arrow
between them, `![v1](before.png) → ![v2](after.png)` (or `->`). Register `imagePairs` with the
same `resolve` as `markdownEmbeds` and that paragraph is one `MediaCompare`, tagged by the two
alts, or Before and After where an alt is only the file's name. Anywhere else, in a sentence or
with a third image, the images stay images: the author chose the pair, so never pair images for
them. Under an `AttachmentViewerProvider` a click opens the document's pairs in the viewer, in
reading order.

## Diagrams

A changed diagram is compared as a picture too: `MermaidCompare` draws both versions of a mermaid
source side by side, and colours what changed, the participant renamed or the node added, rather
than leaving the reader to spot it. Use it where the two sources are at hand, such as a review of a
document's versions; a source diff is one toggle away, and is what shows until both are drawn.
A diagram on its own opens in the viewer from its block's toolbar, to be zoomed as an image is.
