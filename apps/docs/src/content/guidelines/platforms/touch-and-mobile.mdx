## Hit areas

Apple gives a control on iOS 44 × 44pt by default and 28 × 28pt at the least, and counts the space
between controls as much as their size
([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility));
WCAG asks for 24px ([2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html),
AA) and 44px ([2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html), AAA).
@orz/ui takes {touchSpacing}px on a coarse pointer, through its `touch:` variant, and spends it on
what keeps a finger on the right target rather than on drawing a dense screen larger:

- Every control keeps the size its style draws it at, and takes a transparent `::after`
  {touchSpacing}px square, centred on it, as its hit area.
- Neighbouring targets stand {touchSpacing}px apart, centre to centre, so no two hit areas overlap.
- Only what has no other way to make that room changes size: a row a finger taps, a strip of small
  controls along the edge of a box that clips, and a field, whose text grows to
  {font(classes.input, coarse)}px.

| Control | Pointer | Touch, drawn | Touch, hit area |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Button`, every size | {range(...["lg", "default", "sm", "xs", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => px(button(size))))} | {same(...["lg", "default", "sm", "xs", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => touch(button(size))))} | {same(...["lg", "default", "sm", "xs", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => hit(button(size))))} |
| `Switch` ({width(classes.switch)} × {px(classes.switch)}), `Checkbox`, `RadioGroupItem` ({same(px(classes.checkbox), px(classes.radioGroupItem))}) | — | {same(touch(classes.switch), touch(classes.checkbox), touch(classes.radioGroupItem))} | {same(hit(classes.switch), hit(classes.checkbox), hit(classes.radioGroupItem))} |
| `Tabs` trigger | {px(classes.tabsTrigger)} | {touch(classes.tabsTrigger)} | {hit(classes.tabsTrigger)} |

| Part | Pointer | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| `Input`, `HighlightedInput` (so `QueryBox`'s field) | {same(px(classes.input), px(classes.highlightedInput))} | {same(touch(classes.input), touch(classes.highlightedInput))}, at {font(classes.input, coarse)}px text |
| `FilterMenu`'s search field | {px(classes.filterBox)} | {touch(classes.filterBox)}, at {font(classes.filterBox, coarse)}px text |
| `Select` trigger, default / `sm` | {px(classes.selectTrigger)} / {px(classes.selectTrigger, { size: "sm" })} | {px(classes.selectTrigger, coarse)} / {px(classes.selectTrigger, { ...coarse, size: "sm" })} |
| `DropdownMenu` items, `Select` items, `FilterMenu` rows | {same(px(classes.dropdownMenuItem), px(classes.selectItem), px(classes.filterOption))} | {same(touch(classes.dropdownMenuItem), touch(classes.selectItem), touch(classes.filterOption))} |
| `QueryBox` suggestions | {px(classes.queryOption)} | {touch(classes.queryOption)} |
| `RadioGroup` rows | {gap(classes.radioGroup)} apart | {gap(classes.radioGroup, coarse)} apart, centres {gap(classes.radioGroup, coarse) + px(classes.radioGroupItem)} apart |

Sizes are in CSS pixels. "Pointer" is what a mouse gets, and what the [Density](/guidelines/foundations/density/)
page's sizes are. A `Select` trigger is as tall as a field, so the two line up in a form; its text
stays the size it is.

`touch:` follows the primary pointer, so a touchscreen laptop driven by its trackpad keeps the

inside it, which is how the **Touch** button above previews a phone on a desk.

### Your own controls

A control you build yourself takes the same room the same way:

- A row that is tapped is at least {touchSpacing}px tall: `touch:min-h-touch`.
- A small control in a dense row keeps its size and adds the hit area: `relative touch:hit-area`.
  The `::after` belongs to the positioned element, so `relative` (or any positioning) is required.
  On touch the control has no baseline and lines up with text by its middle, so a flex or grid row
  that lines it up with `items-baseline` adds `touch:items-center`. It also keeps the room its
  neighbours need, as the next section says.
- A field takes `touch:h-field-touch`, the height its 16px text needs, as
  [Text fields](#text-fields) says.
- Anything else that changes for a finger, such as a keyboard shortcut hint, uses `touch:` too, not
  Tailwind's `pointer-coarse:`, which no `data-pointer` reaches.
- Tools that hover reveals hide only under `can-hover:`, which holds where the primary pointer can
  hover, so they stay shown on a phone and under a fine pointer that cannot hover. A `data-pointer`
  ancestor sets it too.

### Spacing between targets

**On touch no two neighbouring targets stand closer than {touchSpacing}px, centre to centre.** Two
that stand side by side, each narrower than that, or one above the other, each shorter, are at least
that far apart, so their hit areas do not overlap: a tap on one never lands on the other, and which
comes first in the DOM decides nothing.

@orz/ui's parts keep that room themselves, so a row or a toolbar of them writes nothing for touch:

- An icon `Button`, and so a `ToolbarButton` and every `⋯`, reserves the rest of {touchSpacing}px
  beside it as margin. A margin you give it in `className` (`ml-auto` to push it to a bar's end)
  replaces that room on its side.
- A text `Button` is at least {same(...["lg", "default", "sm", "xs"].map((size) => leastWidth(button(size), coarse)))}px wide: it is as wide as its words, which CSS
  cannot read to leave the rest beside it, so a short label is drawn that wide. A segment of a
  `SegmentedControl` and a tab are at least that wide too, since their faces join.
- A `Toggle` keeps the room above and below it, so the lines of a set of chips that wraps stand
  apart, and is at least {touchSpacing}px wide.
- An `InfoPopover`'s (i) keeps the room above and below it, so the line of the label it stands after
  is {touchSpacing}px tall.
- A `ListItem` that holds a control or is one, a menu's item and a tree's row are at least
  {touchSpacing}px tall, so their targets stand that far from the next row's; a `RadioGroup` spaces
  its rows the same.
- A strip of small controls along the edge of a box that clips, such as a `Card` header that
  holds an action or a link, a `Pane` toolbar or title bar, a framed `Tabs` bar or a viewer's
  toolbar, is at least {touchSpacing}px tall, its controls centred in it.

With that room, fewer commands fit on a `Toolbar` at the same width. It hides no words on touch,
since no tooltip would say them under a finger: a command that does not fit moves into its `⋯`
whole, words and all ([Toolbars](/guidelines/patterns/toolbars/)).

A small control you draw yourself reserves the room the same way: `touch:mx-hit-xs` beside it for
an `xs` one, `hit-sm` for an `sm` one, `hit` for a default one, `hit-lg` for a large one. A stack of
them outside a `ListItem` gives each row `touch:min-h-touch`, and a row of buttons that wraps keeps
its lines apart by twice the token, `touch:gap-y-[calc(2*var(--spacing-hit))]` for default ones.
The tokens follow the style's control sizes, so the centres stand {touchSpacing}px apart in every
style.

Four cases may stand closer:

- **A control and its own label.** A `Checkbox`, `RadioGroupItem` or `Switch` and the text that
  toggles it do the same thing, so these controls reserve no room beside them. A link in that text
  is another target: keep it away from the control, or make the whole row the label.
- **A small control beside a target at least {touchSpacing}px across in the direction they
  neighbour**: its width for two side by side, its height for one above the other, such as a row's
  link, a tree row or a field. The small one's hit area may take a strip of the large one, which
  keeps at least {touchSpacing}px of its own.
- **Marks placed by data on a track**, such as a seek bar's chapter marks under its thumb. Where
  they stand is the data's, not the layout's.
- **Links in running text.** A link inside a sentence stands where the words put it, and the line
  height of the text decides how far it is from a link on the next line, as WCAG's inline exception
  allows ([2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). A list
  of links on their own lines is not running text: its rows take `touch:min-h-touch`.

Inside a box that clips or scrolls (`overflow-hidden`, `overflow-auto`), a hit area that crosses the
box's edge is cut off there; it never makes the box scroll. An icon button's own room already keeps
its hit area inside the box at the side. Above and below a control, and beside one that keeps no
room of its own, leave the rest of the hit area between the control and the edge: give the strip
that holds them `touch:min-h-touch` and centre them in it, or pad by the token of the control's
size (`touch:pr-hit-xs` beside an `xs` one). @orz/ui's own frames, bars and table rows already leave
it for their controls.

  <Do>
    Keep the default sizes and let a coarse pointer add the hit areas and the room between them. One
    layout serves the mouse and the thumb.
  </Do>
  <Dont>
    Draw controls larger for touch, or branch on screen width to do it: the room between targets is

## Text fields

- Fields are 16px text on touch at every width: `Input`, `Textarea`, `HighlightedInput` and
  `FilterMenu`'s search field all are. Safari zooms the page into a field whose text is smaller
  ([how Safari decides](https://thingsthemselves.com/no-input-zoom-in-safari-on-iphone-the-pixel-perfect-way/)),
  and leaves it zoomed after. A field you build takes `text-base md:text-ui touch:text-base`, and
  `h-control touch:h-field-touch` for the height that text needs, so it lines up with ours.
- Give a field the keyboard it needs: `type="email"`, `inputMode="numeric"`, `autoComplete`, and
  `enterKeyHint` (`"search"`, `"send"`, `"next"`) for the label on the return key.
- Do not focus a field on arrival on a phone; that raises the keyboard over a page the person came
  to read. Ask `useMediaQuery(POINTER_FINE)` first, as
  [Desktop & pointer](/guidelines/platforms/desktop-and-pointer/) shows.
- Never turn off pinch zoom (`maximum-scale=1`, `user-scalable=no`): people who need larger text
  zoom ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)).

## Safe areas

A phone's screen has a notch or an island at the top, rounded corners, and a home indicator at the
bottom; in landscape the notch is on a side. With `viewport-fit=cover` in the viewport meta tag the
page draws edge to edge, under all of them, and each edge that holds content pads itself by the
matching inset:

```tsx
<header className="sticky top-0 pt-[env(safe-area-inset-top)] pl-[max(1rem,env(safe-area-inset-left))] pr-[max(1rem,env(safe-area-inset-right))]">
<footer className="fixed inset-x-0 bottom-0 pb-[max(0.5rem,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]">
```

`max()` keeps the usual padding where the inset is zero, which is everywhere but a phone. A
background reaches the edge; only the content moves in.

`Sheet` and `Toaster` pad their own edges already. A `className` padding on `SheetContent` adds to
the inset rather than replacing it; a `Toaster` given its own `offset` or `mobileOffset` replaces
the inset with it, so add the inset there yourself. What is left to the app is its own fixed and
sticky bars and its page gutters; anything that sticks under a header padded this way is offset by
the same `env(safe-area-inset-top)`.

## The on-screen keyboard

A keyboard opening shrinks only the visual viewport: the page keeps its height, and a sticky
`bottom: 0` lands behind the keyboard. Two hooks keep the page in the strip that is left:

- **`useVisualViewportInsets()`**, mounted once at the app's root, publishes `--vv-top` and
  `--vv-bottom` on `<html>`. A sticky edge rides the keyboard with
  `bottom: var(--vv-bottom, 0px)`, so a composer stays right above it.
- **`useScrollInsets({ top, bottom })`** declares the page's sticky header and composer, so every
  `scrollIntoView`, and the anchor and step landings of `revealBlock`, stop clear of them and of
  the keyboard.

```tsx
import { useScrollInsets } from "@orz/ui/hooks/scroll-insets";
import { useVisualViewportInsets } from "@orz/ui/hooks/viewport-chrome";

function Thread() {
  const header = useRef<HTMLElement>(null);
  const composer = useRef<HTMLFormElement>(null);
  useVisualViewportInsets();
  useScrollInsets({ top: [header], bottom: [composer] });
  return (
    <>
      <header ref={header} className="sticky top-0">…</header>
      <main>…</main>
      <form
        ref={composer}
        className="sticky"
        style={{ bottom: "var(--vv-bottom, 0px)" }}
      >
        …
      </form>
    </>
  );
}
```

A `Dialog` sits mid-screen with the keyboard under it; keep one that takes input to a field or two,
so its buttons stay above the keyboard. Anything longer is a page or a `Sheet`.

## Auto-hiding header

On a phone a sticky header is a large share of a small screen. On a long page that is read by
scrolling, a thread or a log, `useAutoHideHeader(header, enabled)` slides it away as the reader
scrolls down and back as they scroll up, the way Safari's toolbar does.

```tsx
import { useElementHeight } from "@orz/ui/hooks/use-element-height";
import { SM_UP, useMediaQuery } from "@orz/ui/hooks/use-media-query";
import { belowHeader, useAutoHideHeader } from "@orz/ui/hooks/viewport-chrome";

const header = useRef<HTMLElement>(null);
useAutoHideHeader(header, !useMediaQuery(SM_UP), pathname);
const height = useElementHeight(() => header.current, 56);

<header
  ref={header}
  data-header-follow=""
  className="sticky"
  style={{ top: belowHeader(0) }}
>
…
<h2 data-header-follow="" className="sticky" style={{ top: belowHeader(height - 1) }}>
```

- Enable it on a phone only; on a wider screen the header costs little and moving it distracts.
- Mount it once, in the app's shell, so whether the header moves never depends on which page the
  reader is on. Pass the route's path as its third argument: each new page then arrives with the
  header in view, as a full load would.
- A layer pinned under the header (a list's group heading, a toolbar) takes
  `top: belowHeader(offset)` and `data-header-follow` too, or a strip of page opens between the two
  while the header moves. Pin it one pixel under the header's height, so it tucks under the header's
  border: at some zoom levels Firefox snaps the two to different device-pixel rows and leaves a
  hairline of page between them.
- Only the reader's own scrolling moves it. A jump to an anchor keeps it where it is, and it settles
  fully in or out when scrolling stops.
- Do not use it on a short page, a form, or a page whose header holds the action people came for:
  that action then disappears under their thumb.

## Gestures

| Gesture | Who owns it | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| Swipe from the left or right edge | The system: back and forward | Nothing within 20px or so of the edge, so the back swipe always works |
| Pull down at the top | The browser: refresh | Leaves the page's overscroll alone; `overscroll-contain` on a panel that scrolls inside the page |
| Pinch | The browser: zoom | Never disabled; a canvas that zooms its own content (an image viewer) takes it only inside itself |
| Long press | The system: select text, preview a link | Nothing a control does not also do: a long press that selects a row repeats the row's checkbox, and a [`SelectionBar`](/guidelines/patterns/actions-and-buttons/#acting-on-a-selection) leads back out |
| Swipe on a toast, a row | The app | Only as a shortcut: the toast also leaves on its own, the row's action is also in its menu, a delete still offers **Undo** |
| Drag a row by its handle | The app | Only from the handle: anywhere else on the row the page scrolls, and the row's pair of move buttons does the same with taps ([Reordering](/guidelines/patterns/reordering/)) |

Every gesture an app adds has a control that does the same thing with one tap, and anything that
follows a path or needs two fingers works with a single pointer too
([WCAG 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)). A tap acts when
the finger lifts, not when it lands, so a finger that lands on the wrong control can slide off;
@orz/ui's buttons already do.

In the example at the top, archive a conversation from its `⋯` menu: the toast that says so can be
swiped away, leaves on its own, and holds the **Undo**.

A selectable tree starts selecting from the row's `⋯` menu, its **Select**, since a link's long
press is the system's; after the first row a tap toggles a row instead of going, and the menu's
**Clear selection** or the `SelectionBar`'s brings the taps back to going.

`Dialog` and `Sheet` lock the page behind them while they are open, and let a scroller inside them,
a code diff included, scroll on its own.
