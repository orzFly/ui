## Hit areas

44 × 44 is Apple's minimum for a touch target
([Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility));
WCAG's floor is 24px ([2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)).
@orz/ui meets the larger one on a coarse pointer through its `touch:` variant, in one of two ways:
a control that has room is drawn larger, and a control that sits in a dense row keeps its drawn
size and widens its hit area with a transparent `::after`.

| Control | Pointer | Touch, drawn | Touch, hit area |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Button` default | {px(button())} | {touch(button())} | {hit(button())} |
| `Button` `lg` | {px(button("lg"))} | {touch(button("lg"))} | {hit(button("lg"))} |
| `Button` `xs`, `sm`, and the `icon` sizes | {range(...["xs", "sm", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => px(button(size))))} | {same(...["xs", "sm", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => touch(button(size))))} | {same(...["xs", "sm", "icon", "icon-sm", "icon-xs", "icon-lg"].map((size) => hit(button(size))))} |
| `Switch` ({width(classes.switch)} × {px(classes.switch)}), `Checkbox`, `RadioGroupItem` ({same(px(classes.checkbox), px(classes.radioGroupItem))}) | — | {same(touch(classes.switch), touch(classes.checkbox), touch(classes.radioGroupItem))} | {same(hit(classes.switch), hit(classes.checkbox), hit(classes.radioGroupItem))} |
| `Input`, `HighlightedInput` (so `QueryBox`'s field) | {same(px(classes.input), px(classes.highlightedInput))} | {same(touch(classes.input), touch(classes.highlightedInput))}, at {font(classes.input, coarse)}px text | {same(hit(classes.input), hit(classes.highlightedInput))} |
| `FilterMenu`'s search field | {px(classes.filterBox)} | {touch(classes.filterBox)}, at {font(classes.filterBox, coarse)}px text | {hit(classes.filterBox)} |
| `Select` trigger, default / `sm` | {px(classes.selectTrigger)} / {px(classes.selectTrigger, { size: "sm" })} | {px(classes.selectTrigger, coarse)} / {px(classes.selectTrigger, { ...coarse, size: "sm" })} | {same(hit(classes.selectTrigger), hit(classes.selectTrigger, { size: "sm" }))} |
| `Tabs` trigger | {px(classes.tabsTrigger)} | {touch(classes.tabsTrigger)} | {hit(classes.tabsTrigger)} |
| `DropdownMenu` items, `Select` items, `FilterMenu` rows | {same(px(classes.dropdownMenuItem), px(classes.selectItem), px(classes.filterOption))} | {same(touch(classes.dropdownMenuItem), touch(classes.selectItem), touch(classes.filterOption))} | {same(hit(classes.dropdownMenuItem), hit(classes.selectItem), hit(classes.filterOption))} |
| `QueryBox` suggestions | {px(classes.queryOption)} | {touch(classes.queryOption)} | {hit(classes.queryOption)} |
| `RadioGroup` rows | {gap(classes.radioGroup)} apart | {gap(classes.radioGroup, coarse)} apart | rows {gap(classes.radioGroup, coarse) + px(classes.radioGroupItem)} apart |

Sizes are in CSS pixels. "Pointer" is what a mouse gets, and what the [Density](/guidelines/foundations/density/)
page's sizes are.

`touch:` follows the primary pointer, so a touchscreen laptop driven by its trackpad keeps the
pointer sizes. `data-pointer="coarse"` or `"fine"` on an element forces either set on everything
inside it, which is how the **Touch** button above previews a phone on a desk.

### Your own controls

A control you build yourself takes the same sizes the same way:

- A row that is tapped grows with `touch:min-h-11` (44px).
- A small control in a dense row keeps its size and adds the hit area: `relative touch:hit-area`.
  The `::after` belongs to the positioned element, so `relative` (or any positioning) is required.

### Spacing between targets

A hit area wider than its control overlaps its neighbours. Where two overlap, the later one in the
DOM takes the tap, so:

- Leave 8px or more between two controls that do different things, and more between a control and
  something destructive.
- A link in text right next to a `Switch` or `Checkbox` loses the taps near the control. Keep the
  link away from it, or make the whole row the control's label.
- Inside a box with `overflow-hidden`, a hit area that crosses the box's edge is cut off there. Pad
  the box, or move the control in from the edge.

## Text fields

- Fields are 16px text on touch at every width: `Input`, `Textarea`, `HighlightedInput` and
  `FilterMenu`'s search field all are. Safari zooms the page into a field whose text is smaller
  ([how Safari decides](https://thingsthemselves.com/no-input-zoom-in-safari-on-iphone-the-pixel-perfect-way/)),
  and leaves it zoomed after. A field you build takes `text-base md:text-ui touch:text-base`.
- Give a field the keyboard it needs: `type="email"`, `inputMode="numeric"`, `autoComplete`, and
  `enterKeyHint` (`"search"`, `"send"`, `"next"`) for the label on the return key.
- Do not focus a field on arrival on a phone; that raises the keyboard over a page the person came
  to read. Ask `useMediaQuery(POINTER_FINE)` first, as
  [Desktop & pointer](/guidelines/platforms/desktop-and-pointer/) shows.
- Never turn off pinch zoom (`maximum-scale=1`, `user-scalable=no`): people who need larger text
  zoom ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)).

## Safe areas

A phone's screen has a notch or an island at the top, rounded corners, and a home indicator at the
bottom; in landscape the notch is on a side. With `viewport-fit=cover` in the viewport meta tag the
page draws edge to edge, under all of them, and each edge that holds content pads itself by the
matching inset:

```tsx
<header className="sticky top-0 pt-[env(safe-area-inset-top)] pl-[max(1rem,env(safe-area-inset-left))] pr-[max(1rem,env(safe-area-inset-right))]">
<footer className="fixed inset-x-0 bottom-0 pb-[max(0.5rem,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]">
```

`max()` keeps the usual padding where the inset is zero, which is everywhere but a phone. A
background reaches the edge; only the content moves in.

`Sheet` and `Toaster` pad their own edges already. A `className` padding on `SheetContent` adds to
the inset rather than replacing it; a `Toaster` given its own `offset` or `mobileOffset` replaces
the inset with it, so add the inset there yourself. What is left to the app is its own fixed and
sticky bars and its page gutters; anything that sticks under a header padded this way is offset by
the same `env(safe-area-inset-top)`.

## The on-screen keyboard

A keyboard opening shrinks only the visual viewport: the page keeps its height, and a sticky
`bottom: 0` lands behind the keyboard. Two hooks keep the page in the strip that is left:

- **`useVisualViewportInsets()`**, mounted once at the app's root, publishes `--vv-top` and
  `--vv-bottom` on `<html>`. A sticky edge rides the keyboard with
  `bottom: var(--vv-bottom, 0px)`, so a composer stays right above it.
- **`useScrollInsets({ top, bottom })`** declares the page's sticky header and composer, so every
  `scrollIntoView`, and the anchor and step landings of `revealBlock`, stop clear of them and of
  the keyboard.

```tsx
import { useScrollInsets } from "@orz/ui/hooks/scroll-insets";
import { useVisualViewportInsets } from "@orz/ui/hooks/viewport-chrome";

function Thread() {
  const header = useRef<HTMLElement>(null);
  const composer = useRef<HTMLFormElement>(null);
  useVisualViewportInsets();
  useScrollInsets({ top: [header], bottom: [composer] });
  return (
    <>
      <header ref={header} className="sticky top-0">…</header>
      <main>…</main>
      <form
        ref={composer}
        className="sticky"
        style={{ bottom: "var(--vv-bottom, 0px)" }}
      >
        …
      </form>
    </>
  );
}
```

A `Dialog` sits mid-screen with the keyboard under it; keep one that takes input to a field or two,
so its buttons stay above the keyboard. Anything longer is a page or a `Sheet`.

## Auto-hiding header

On a phone a sticky header is a large share of a small screen. On a long page that is read by
scrolling, a thread or a log, `useAutoHideHeader(header, enabled)` slides it away as the reader
scrolls down and back as they scroll up, the way Safari's toolbar does.

```tsx
import { SM_UP, useMediaQuery } from "@orz/ui/hooks/use-media-query";
import { useAutoHideHeader } from "@orz/ui/hooks/viewport-chrome";

const header = useRef<HTMLElement>(null);
useAutoHideHeader(header, !useMediaQuery(SM_UP));

<header
  ref={header}
  data-header-follow=""
  className="sticky"
  style={{ top: "calc(var(--vv-top, 0px) - var(--header-hide, 0px))" }}
>
```

- Enable it on a phone only; on a wider screen the header costs little and moving it distracts.
- Only the reader's own scrolling moves it. A jump to an anchor keeps it where it is, and it settles
  fully in or out when scrolling stops.
- Do not use it on a short page, a form, or a page whose header holds the action people came for:
  that action then disappears under their thumb.

## Gestures

| Gesture | Who owns it | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| Swipe from the left or right edge | The system: back and forward | Nothing within 20px or so of the edge, so the back swipe always works |
| Pull down at the top | The browser: refresh | Leaves the page's overscroll alone; `overscroll-contain` on a panel that scrolls inside the page |
| Pinch | The browser: zoom | Never disabled; a canvas that zooms its own content (an image viewer) takes it only inside itself |
| Long press | The system: select text, preview a link | Nothing a control does not also do: a long press that selects a row repeats the row's checkbox |
| Swipe on a toast, a row | The app | Only as a shortcut: the toast also leaves on its own, the row's action is also in its menu, a delete still offers **Undo** |

Every gesture an app adds has a control that does the same thing with one tap, and anything that
follows a path or needs two fingers works with a single pointer too
([WCAG 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)). A tap acts when
the finger lifts, not when it lands, so a finger that lands on the wrong control can slide off;
@orz/ui's buttons already do.

In the example at the top, archive a conversation from its `⋯` menu: the toast that says so can be
swiped away, leaves on its own, and holds the **Undo**.

`Dialog` and `Sheet` lock the page behind them while they are open, and let a scroller inside them,
a code diff included, scroll on its own.

## On each platform

- **Pointer.** The compact sizes from [Density](/guidelines/foundations/density/), hover,
  shortcuts: [Desktop & pointer](/guidelines/platforms/desktop-and-pointer/).
- **Touch.** Everything on this page. Preview it on a desk with the **Touch** button under any

  neither emulates the keyboard, the notch or the system's gestures.
